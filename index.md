## About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Computer Science at [Virginia Tech](https://www.vt.edu/), advised by [Prof. Wenjing Lou](https://www.cnsr.ictas.vt.edu/WJLou.html) in the [Complex Networks and Security Research (CNSR)](https://www.cnsr.ictas.vt.edu/) group. I am a security and privacy researcher working across AI, networked, and computing systems. My work spans LLM and agent security and privacy, including agent privacy, workflow anomaly detection and monitoring, and trustworthy AI training and inference; applied cryptography and privacy, including anonymous authentication, privacy-preserving computation, and hardware-backed roots of trust and attestation (TEEs, TPMs, and SIMs); and network and communication security, including security and privacy in cellular and 5G networks, spam and scam defense, network monitoring, V2X communications, and satellite networks.

A recurring theme in my research is studying security and privacy across interconnected components and layers, and designing practical protocols and mechanisms to address these challenges. I am particularly interested in cross-layer problems, where security and privacy depend on interactions among multiple components, protocols, trust boundaries, and real-world system constraints. My work combines the analysis of these interactions with the design and evaluation of practical solutions that integrate appropriate security mechanisms across different parts of the system.

Before joining Virginia Tech, I received my M.S. in Computer Science from **Arkansas State University** and my B.S. in Communication Engineering from **Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications (BUPT)**. During my Ph.D., I was also a Research Intern at [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/), where I worked on security and privacy for agentic AI systems, mentored by [Dr. Betül Durak](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/people/betuldurak/) and [Dr. Satya Lokam](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/people/satya/).
